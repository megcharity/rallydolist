Guest Recovery

Things will go wrong. A dish takes too long, a drink misses the mark, a guest is simply disappointed. It's not about what we give them — it's about how they feel when they leave. As a manager, that feeling is on you.

Own it

It's your responsibility as a manager to own every guest's satisfaction and experience.

It's your job

Any guest concern, big or small, should be brought to your attention by the team immediately — that's the standard you set. The team's job is to flag it fast. Connecting with that guest, personally, is yours. Don't delegate it and don't wait for it to be escalated twice.

  • The team tells you right away — that's the whole job on their end
  • You go to the guest yourself, not a message passed back through someone else
  • If you're mid-task, finish what you must and go — this comes first
  • A guest should never have to ask twice, or ask someone higher up, to get your attention

Listen before you fix

Most guests just want to know you heard them and that you care.

  • Give the guest your full attention
  • Listen for the problem and the feeling behind it
  • Don't explain or make excuses — own it

Exception — food poisoning or illness claims. Don't apologize for the cause. Say only "I'm sorry to hear you aren't feeling well," and connect them with the General Manager. Never say anything that sounds like Rally accepts fault.

Keep the team in the loop

Recovery is still a team effort — you're leading it, not handling it alone.

  • Tell the bartender, host, kitchen or captain if it affects them, and what to do differently
  • Don't make the guest repeat their story to multiple people
  • Thank whoever flagged it — that's the behavior you want every time

Magic moments

Guest recovery isn't only fixing problems. It's noticing.

A small, personal surprise because someone was paying attention to what the guest shared. A guest loves sweets but can't eat another bite — send a cookie home for later. A pregnant guest says a glass of bubbly sounds amazing — surprise them with a non-alcoholic pour.

As the manager, this is your call. You don't need to check with anyone else to comp or add a small gesture. The value isn't the cost — it's that someone noticed and cared. Document it in the shift report.
