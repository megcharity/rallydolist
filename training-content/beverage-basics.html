Beer, Wine
+ Cocktails

Our bar program is simple on purpose — but every guest deserves a bartender who knows the basics. This chapter covers what you need to talk about, pour, and make what we serve with confidence. Our menu and specs build on everything here.

Non-negotiable

Every single drink with liquor gets measured with a jigger. Every pour, every time — no free pouring.

Shots, neat pours, highballs, and cocktails included. Measuring keeps every drink consistent for guests and keeps our costs right.
The Rally Standard

You don't need to know everything. You need to know the basics well, ask good questions, and make every drink the same way, every time.

Describe it in three words

Guests don't need a lecture. Pick a style or body, a flavor, and a feeling: "light, citrusy, refreshing." "Bold, dark fruit, smooth." Then ask what they usually like and match it.

Part 1

Beer Basics

Almost every beer is either an ale or a lager. Ales tend to be fuller and fruitier; lagers tend to be crisp and clean. From there, it's mostly about how hoppy (bitter, citrusy, piney) or malty (bready, caramel, roasty) a beer is.

Styles to know

Light lager + pilsnerCrisp, clean, easy-drinking. The safe bet for "something light."Great after a game
Wheat beerSoft, smooth, often hazy, with citrus or banana-clove notes. Low bitterness.For guests who don't like bitter
Pale ale + IPAHop-forward. West Coast IPAs are bitter and piney; hazy IPAs are juicy and tropical.Ask: bitter or juicy?
Amber + brownMalty, toasty, caramel. Balanced, not too bitter.A middle-ground pick
Stout + porterDark and roasty, with coffee or chocolate notes. Dark doesn't always mean heavy or strong.Great with rich food
Sour, cider + seltzerSours are tart and fruity. Cider is made from apples. Seltzer is light and bubbly.Check the allergen sheet for gluten

Pouring a draft

  1. Start with a clean, cold glass — never one that was used for something else.
  2. Hold the glass at a 45° angle under the tap, without touching it.
  3. Open the tap all the way in one motion.
  4. Straighten the glass halfway through to build about an inch of foam.
  5. Close the tap fully. Never let the faucet touch the beer.

Words to know

  • ABV — alcohol by volume; most beers are 4–6%, some IPAs go much higher
  • Draft — beer from the tap, out of a keg
  • Hoppy — bitter, citrusy, piney, or tropical
  • Malty — bready, caramel, toasty
  • Hazy — cloudy, usually soft and juicy
  • Session — lower ABV, made to have a few
Part 2

Wine Basics

You don't need to be a sommelier. Learn a few grapes, and describe wine by body (light, medium, full), sweetness (most of what we pour is dry), and flavor (fruit, earth, oak). That's enough to guide almost any guest.

The big four

WhitesUsually lighter and served cold.Sauvignon Blanc: crisp, citrusy · Pinot Grigio: light, clean · Chardonnay: fuller; buttery if oaked
RedsMore body and tannin — the drying feeling on your tongue.Pinot Noir: light, red fruit · Merlot: soft, medium · Cabernet Sauvignon: full, bold
RoséMade from red grapes with little skin contact. Usually dry, fresh, and fruity.An easy pick for patio days
SparklingBubbles from a second fermentation. Ranges from dry to off-dry.Prosecco: fruity, easy · Champagne: from France, crisp, toasty · Cava: Spain, crisp

Serving wine

  1. Pour our standard pour every time — check the spec sheet for our pour size.
  2. Hold the glass by the stem so the wine stays cool.
  3. Whites, rosé, and sparkling are served cold; reds slightly cool.
  4. Sparkling: keep a hand on the cork, point it away from people, and twist the bottle, not the cork.
  5. Recap, pump, and date every open bottle. Taste-test anything open over 7 days.

Words to know

  • Dry — not sweet
  • Body — how heavy it feels: light, medium, full
  • Tannin — the drying feeling, mostly in reds
  • Oaked — aged in oak: vanilla, toast, butter
  • Acidity — the crisp, mouthwatering quality
  • By the glass / bottle — know which wines we offer each way

The best wine question: "What do you usually like to drink?" Then match their answer to something on our list.

Part 3

Cocktail Basics

Most cocktails are a balance of three things: spirit, sweet, and sour or bitter. Measure every ingredient with a jigger and follow our specs exactly — that's how the drink tastes the same no matter who makes it.

The spirits

VodkaClean and neutral. Takes on whatever it's mixed with.
GinVodka flavored with botanicals — mostly juniper. Herbal and bright.
RumMade from sugarcane. Light rum is clean; aged or dark rum is richer.
Tequila + mezcalMade from agave. Blanco is bright; reposado and añejo are aged and smoother. Mezcal is smoky.
WhiskeyBourbon is sweeter (corn); rye is spicier; Scotch is from Scotland and can be smoky.
Liqueurs + bittersSweetened, flavored spirits (like orange liqueur) and concentrated bitters that add depth in small amounts.

Three families

SoursSpirit + citrus + sweet. Shaken.Margarita · Daiquiri · Whiskey Sour
Spirit-forwardMostly spirit, little or no juice. Stirred.Old Fashioned · Manhattan · Martini
HighballsSpirit + a mixer, built right in the glass over ice.Vodka Soda · Gin + Tonic · Paloma

Shake or stir?

  1. Shake anything with citrus, juice, cream, or egg — it mixes, chills, and adds texture.
  2. Stir drinks that are all spirits — it chills and dilutes while keeping them smooth and clear.
  3. Build highballs in the glass: ice, spirit, then mixer.
  4. Use fresh ice in the glass, never the ice from the shaker.
  5. Garnish last — it adds aroma, not just looks.

Words to know

  • Neat — straight from the bottle, no ice
  • On the rocks — over ice
  • Up — chilled, strained, served without ice
  • Dirty — with olive brine (martinis)
  • Dry — less vermouth (martinis)
  • Twist — a citrus peel garnish
  • Well vs. call — house spirit vs. a brand the guest names
  • Spec — our recipe: every ingredient and amount

Serving Responsibly

This is the most important basic of all — and it's the law.

  • Check IDs. Anyone who looks under 30 gets carded, every time. No valid ID, no drink.
  • Pay attention. Watch for signs of intoxication: slurred speech, loud or clumsy behavior, glassy eyes.
  • Pace and offer water. Count rounds, offer water and food, and suggest NA options.
  • Never serve a guest who is visibly intoxicated. Stay calm and kind, and get the MOD involved right away.
