Guest Recovery

Things will go wrong. A dish takes too long, a drink misses the mark, or a guest is simply disappointed. It's not about what we give them. It's about how they feel when they leave.

Any concern, big or small: tell a manager right away.

If a guest mentions anything that feels off, even something minor, let a manager know immediately. The manager will touch base with that guest. Your job is to make sure they know.

Listen before you fix

Most guests just want to know we heard them and that we care.

  • Give the guest your full attention.
  • Listen for the problem and the feeling behind it.
  • Don't explain or make excuses. Own it.

Keep the team in the loop

Recovery is a team effort.

  • Tell the manager what happened and what the guest said.
  • Let the bartender, host, or kitchen know if it affects them.
  • Don't make the guest repeat their story to multiple people.

Magic moments

Hospitality isn't only fixing problems. It's noticing.

A magic moment is a small, personal surprise that happens because you were paying attention to something the guest shared. For example:

  • A guest loves sweets but can't eat another bite. Send a cookie home for later.
  • A pregnant guest says a glass of bubbly sounds amazing. Surprise them with a non-alcoholic sparkling pour.

Talk to a manager before giving anything. The value isn't the cost. It's the feeling that someone noticed and cared.

Before you call it done

  • The guest feels heard.
  • A manager knows and has checked in with the guest.
  • We did something about it, not just apologized.
  • Someone went back to make sure it worked.
