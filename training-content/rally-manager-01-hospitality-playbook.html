Service vs. Hospitality

At Rally, we embody Hand-Holding Hospitality: service and genuine warmth working together, so every guest feels guided, cared for, and glad they chose to be here. That's what turns a great visit into one people can't stop talking about.

Service · what you do
  • Taking orders correctly
  • Delivering food and drinks on time
  • Executing systems and standards
  • Detailing and resetting sections
  • Following checklists and SOPs
  • Moving with urgency so guests never wait longer than they should
  • Knowing the menu inside and out
Hospitality · how they feel
  • Making guests feel seen and valued
  • Anticipating needs before they're spoken
  • Creating a sense of belonging and warmth
  • Greeting, guiding, and connecting
  • Being fully present in every interaction

We acknowledge every guest

Anyone within two feet: eye contact, energy, smile.

  • Eye contact and a genuine smile
  • Speak up with confidence. A greeting only lands if it's heard
  • Give full attention, every single time
  • Mid-task? Pause, smile and say “I'll be right with you!” Then finish what you were doing

We see, we do

Stay proactive. Move as a team.

  • Spot something that needs doing? Do it right away
  • See a teammate stretched? Step in without being asked
  • Notice a guest who could use help? Go to them
  • Keep the whole venue in view, not just your section

We bring the energy

Be the Cruise Director.

We work as a team

One floor. One goal. No ego.

  • Communicate proactively. If something changes, say so right away
  • Treat every role with equal respect: host, busser, barback, GM
  • Celebrate each other's wins and step in when a teammate needs support
  • Handle interpersonal moments privately, promptly and professionally
  • Over-communicate. Silence is never the plan
Tap to flip back
The Cruise Director effect: a cruise director holds the whole experience together. They know what's happening, they make sure every guest knows too, and they set the tone without waiting to be asked. One manager who's fully present can lift the entire room. Guests feed off your energy.

Magic Moments

Going the extra mile, both proactively and reactively. When things go well, make them better. When something needs turning around, move fast and with care.

Offense · elevate
  • Notice a milestone and mark it
  • Recommend the next drink before the glass is empty
  • Bring something small and unexpected
  • Connect a first-timer to the Rally community
Defense · recover with the four A's
  • Apologize for how it affected them, even if it wasn't our fault
  • Acknowledge it: if the guest feels there's a problem, there is one
  • Act: loop in a manager without hesitation and fix it fast
  • Assess: go back after the fix to make sure they feel taken care of
What a Magic Moment looks like
  • A guest mentions in passing it's their birthday, and a candle appears
  • A regular sits down, and their usual order is already on its way
  • A first-timer finishes their first court session, and you introduce them to a regular
  • An order needs to be remade, and it comes back fast with something extra

Find a way to say yes

A well-timed yes builds loyalty. These are the kinds of yeses we look for:

  • Extend a reservation for free if no other guests are waiting for the space
  • Accommodate a party size increase, and search for the solution
  • Go off-menu for a guest who needs something (check with the kitchen first)
  • Make thoughtful exceptions for regulars and VIPs where appropriate
  • Walk guests to the restroom instead of pointing
  • Offer water for guests' dogs
  • Be a helpful concierge: directions, suggestions, local knowledge
  • When you bend a rule, let the guest know you're doing it for them
As a manager: your team is told to ask their direct report before saying no to a guest. That's you. Make it easy for them to ask, and look for the yes first. The answer is often yes.
