Of course, we have our checklists and daily responsibilities — ordering, inventory, opening and closing tasks, and more. But your job as a manager is bigger than that. You have a much larger responsibility for our people, our guests, and the performance of the business.

Your Job as a Manager

Four things that are now on you, every shift: the team, the guests, the business, and what's coming next.

If your team is underperforming, that's on you. Their performance is on you. Anything that goes right or wrong on your shift is on you.

Team Development, Accountability & the Shift

It's our responsibility to make sure the team knows the menu, specials, service standards, and how to sell.

Think of it like coaching a soccer team. You wouldn't put everyone on the field without a plan or coaching. That means actively managing and coaching throughout the entire shift — not just doing lineup and then letting everyone run on their own. We need to be constantly observing, redirecting, recognizing great performance, and giving feedback in the moment. That feedback can't only come from a GM — it's on you, every shift.

Coach two things every shift: selling and guest experience. Is the team suggesting something, not just taking orders? Are they making guests feel welcomed and remembered? Catch it in the moment — recognize it when it's happening, redirect it when it's not.

We also need to actively direct the shift. If there's downtime, what needs attention?

  • Is the bar clean?
  • Are syrups prepped?
  • Are guest areas looking great?
  • What can we get ahead on?
The team should always know their priorities. Setting and managing those priorities, shift after shift, is your job.

Guest Satisfaction

We own the guest experience.

Connect with guests throughout every shift — introducing yourself, welcoming them, thanking them for coming, and anticipating their needs. Think of this like a manager "table touch." Connecting with our guests is a required part of managing the floor.

Every complaint goes to a manager. Never leave it for an associate to handle. Guest recovery is a critical manager responsibility — when something goes wrong, step in, listen, resolve the issue, and make sure that guest leaves feeling taken care of.

Getting first-time guests back is on you, too. A great first visit is only half the job — it's our responsibility to make sure that guest has a reason to come back. Notice who's new, make the introduction count, and give them something specific to return for before they leave.

Business Performance

We're responsible for the business, not just for showing up.

Make sure your team is selling and looking for opportunities throughout the shift — that's on you to set up and reinforce, not something that happens on its own. Constantly ask: What could be better? Where are we losing opportunities? What can we improve?

Maintaining the business isn't enough. Our responsibility isn't simply to maintain the business while we're on duty. We should be actively looking for ways to make it better.

Planning Ahead

Strong shifts start before the shift begins.

Look ahead at reservations, programming, events, staffing and potential pressure points, and build battle plans when needed. The goal is to anticipate problems instead of reacting to them once they happen.
