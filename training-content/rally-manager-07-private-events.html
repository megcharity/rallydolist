Rally prides itself on its events. We host everything from birthdays to corporate outings and tournaments right here on our courts. Review our event packages so you know exactly what's included, and bring the same service and hospitality to every event that we bring to every guest.

Sales sells a vision. Operations makes it real. Every event we sell is a promise about how a space will look, feel and function on the day. Guests don't see the sales conversation or the BEO; they see the room.

The BEO is the contract

Not a suggestion. The BEO is the agreed terms of the event.

  • Guest count and layout, exactly as specified
  • Timing (start, service points and breakdown), exactly as specified
  • Specialty requests and add-ons fully accounted for, not approximated
  • Any discrepancy flagged before setup begins, never discovered mid-event
If you don't know, ask. BEOs are available to everyone on Tripleseat, and the BEO meeting happens two weeks in advance. Each BEO lists the sales and event team member. Ask your questions before the day.

What “right” looks like

The bar for every event, whatever its size.

Timing

  • Setup complete and inspected 15–30 minutes before doors open
  • We never want to scramble at guest arrival

Layout accuracy

  • Matches the floorplan exactly
  • “Close enough” isn't a standard we hold

Stations & signage

  • Placed, stocked and functional
  • No setting up buffets as guests arrive, even if food comes out later

AV

  • Tested, not just set up
  • “It's plugged in” isn't the same as “it works”

The feel

  • The room reflects the vision that was sold, not just the headcount that was booked

Event day, step by step

Every event, every time. No exceptions for “small” ones.

Pre-event walkthrough
  1. Walk the floorplan against the roomConfirm every table, station and placement matches what's on paper.
  2. Test all AV and techBefore guests arrive, not after someone asks for the mic.
  3. Confirm specialty itemsFlorals, signage, favors, a gifts table: whatever the BEO calls for.
  4. Review the BEO with the on-site leadA final read-through on arrival catches what a first pass misses.
  5. Confirm timing 1 hour beforeGet service and kitchen on the same clock before the first guest walks in.
Team arrival to doors open
  1. Battle plan readyPrepped so when the team walks in they can see their duties and complete them before the pre-shift meeting.
  2. Pre-shift, 30 minutes beforePut the time on the battle plan. Gather everyone promptly and review the event in full.
  3. Final touches onlyAfter pre-shift, only final touches: lighting candles, turning on heat lamps.
  4. Ready before guests arriveThe team greets guests as they walk in, hand-passing hors d'oeuvres and so on.

During the event

Consistency the client can feel, even if they can't name it.

  • Course and station timing stays on the BEO schedule unless the client requests a change
  • Staff are responsive but not intrusive. Read the room before stepping in
  • Last-minute client requests get acknowledged immediately, even if the answer takes a few minutes
  • Problems get solved quietly. Guests shouldn't see the fix happening
Before the POC leaves: find them in person, get their feedback, and fill in the Events section of the shift report (Chapter 6). Look for moments to tell event guests what Rally offers beyond today (Chapter 2).

When something doesn't match the BEO

A clear path, not a guess in the moment.

  1. Flag it immediatelyTell the on-site lead. Don't wait to see if it resolves itself; it rarely does before doors open.
  2. If it affects guests, fix it firstDocument after. The guest's experience doesn't wait on paperwork.
  3. Guest wants to add something? Add it yourselfIf they want to add anything to their packet, you're empowered to add it directly to the check — no need to loop in sales. Want to add a court? Apply a 20% upsell on the current court price.
  4. Document every deviationFor the post-event debrief, so the same gap doesn't happen again.
Common setup mistakes: treating guest count as the only spec that matters · setting up to the floorplan without checking it against the actual room · leaving AV “set up” without testing it · assuming a small deviation won't be noticed. Clients notice.
