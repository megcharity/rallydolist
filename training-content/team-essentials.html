Who we are

How we serve

Rally service is professional but casual, conversational rather than scripted, and built on six points: hospitality, presentation, knowledge, teamwork, critical thinking and technical proficiency.

The Two-Foot Rule. Acknowledge every guest within two feet of you with eye contact, energy and a smile. Mid-task? Pause, smile and say "I'll be right with you!" They are guests, never "customers."

The non-negotiables

  1. Start and finish every interaction with a smile.
  2. Use names — guests' and teammates'.
  3. Never, ever interrupt. Approach silently, wait for acknowledgement; if needed, rest a hand on the table and wait for eye contact.
  4. Manicure the table before you leave it: remove anything no longer needed.
  5. Say hello while giving guests the right of way.
  6. Guests never hear talk about the business or your personal life.

Also expected:

  • Never leave a guest with a flat "no" — offer an alternative
  • Escort guests partway ("let me show you the way"), point with your whole hand
  • Don't announce your next move ("I'll ring that in and be right back")
  • Never sit, kneel, lean or crouch at a table
  • Let guests offer a handshake — don't initiate one

These are guidelines, not a script. Read the table and mirror it: a date night, a business happy hour and a group of first-time birthday players each want a different pace.

Teamwork and the 3 C's

Every guest is everyone's guest. If food is up, run it; if a table is dirty, clear it; if a guest flags you, help or find the answer.

  • Communicate: can't do it yourself? Hand it off clearly so nothing slips.
  • Circulate: keep moving, look for one need at every table, never congregate with teammates on the floor.
  • Consolidate: never walk empty-handed — take dirties in, bring food or supplies out.
The five critical moments — when guests want service fastest
  1. They sit down → drink
  2. A course finishes → manicure and crumb the table
  3. A drink gets low → refill
  4. Food arrives and something's missing → quality check
  5. It's time to go → payment
Sequence of service and time standards

Every tab starts with a pre-authorized card, before the first order goes in.

Step one: pre-authorize a card. QR orders: Toast handles this automatically. Handheld orders: get a card and pre-authorize before ringing anything in. Either way, the guest can still close out on that card or pay another way at the end.

Ordering with Toast Mobile Order & Pay. Every court, table and bar seat has a QR code — guests scan, order and can add to the same tab as their group. Technical basics to practice

  • Glassware: hold by the bottom third or the stem, never near the rim; more than two drinks always goes on a tray. Place on the guest's right; water goes farthest right. Coasters with the first round. Never serve in a warm glass.
  • Plates: carry three at most, by the edge, no "butterfly" carries. Serve from the left, clear from the right, at hip level — never "helicopter" over a guest. Hot plates for hot food, cold for cold.
  • Clearing: ask the guest if it's okay to clear their empty dishes. Never stack plates on or over the table. Crumb onto a plate, never the floor.
  • Leftovers: bring boxes to the table; box for the guest only if they ask.
Alcohol, complaints and safety

These are the rules with legal or safety consequences — no judgment calls here, and always loop in the manager on duty (MOD).

Responsible alcohol service. It is illegal to serve a minor or an intoxicated person, and you can be held personally responsible.

  • Check ID for anyone who looks under 30; no valid photo ID proving 21+, no alcohol. Decline politely and without judgment.
  • Track consumption for each guest, and remember they may have been drinking elsewhere.
  • Watch for signs: slurred speech, glassy eyes, stumbling or spilling, loud or abusive talk, sudden mood swings. Don't mistake a loud personality or a medical issue (diabetes, stroke) for intoxication.
  • Cutting someone off: stop serving, tell the MOD and your teammates so the guest can't order elsewhere. Approach privately and kindly, offer food and non-alcoholic drinks, and don't argue. The MOD will back you up.
  • If it escalates: never physically detain a guest; bring a teammate if they're threatening; call the police if needed. Arrange an Uber or Lyft and strongly discourage driving.

Health and safety

  • Sick? Tell your manager immediately and try to find coverage; a doctor's note is required.
  • Wash your hands any time you enter a restroom, touch your face, cough or sneeze.
  • Note any allergy or dislike a guest mentions and carry it through their whole visit.
  • Report any pest sighting to a manager immediately.
  • On court: never interrupt a rally. If a ball rolls onto another court mid-game, call "Ball" — don't retrieve it yourself. Anyone can call "Let" to stop play for a safety issue.
Pickleball and court basics

Every team member should be able to explain the game and how to book — guests ask constantly, and "I don't know" is the worst answer we can give.

  • The game: a mix of tennis, badminton and ping-pong, played singles or doubles with a paddle and a plastic ball. Traditional scoring goes to 11 and only the serving team scores; rally scoring goes to 21 and every rally scores. Win by 2 either way.
  • Three rules guests ask about most: the serve is underhand, hit below the waist, diagonally past the kitchen line. The serve and the return must each bounce before anyone volleys (the double-bounce rule). No volleys while touching the kitchen — the 7-foot zone by the net.
  • Booking: online via rallypickleball.com (Playbypoint) is preferred; phone or front desk also works, and walk-ins get open courts first come, first served. Reservations open 5 days ahead, in 1- or 1.5-hour blocks. Large groups count as a private event — let a manager know.
  • Equipment: every reservation includes a Rally paddle per player and 2 balls per court. We sell Joola paddles (say "YO-la") and Rhoback apparel at the front desk.
  • Arrival: guests check in at least 10 minutes before court time; first-timers are encouraged to come 30 minutes early to learn the rules.
  • Where to send inquiries: any private event goes to a manager. Corporate wellness programs and private lessons go to the Director of Pickleball. Classes (Rally Rookies, Pickleball Pump) and leagues (Leisure League, Rally Ranks) book online or at the front desk.
