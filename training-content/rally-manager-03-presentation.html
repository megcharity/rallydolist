Presentation

Everything a guest sees, hears and feels at Rally is shaped by us. As a manager, you hold this standard for yourself first and then for your team, every shift, however busy or tired everyone is.

The Rally Standard

Professionalism isn’t about being stiff or formal. It’s showing up in a way that says: we take pride in this place, and we take pride in taking care of you.

How we present ourselves

Guests should be able to tell immediately that you work at Rally and that you're here to take care of them. Rally's vibe is active, elevated and approachable.

Uniform & attire

  • Rally uniform is business casual: sharp, clean and ready
  • No hoodies

Grooming

  • Hair, nails and overall presentation sharp and guest-ready before your shift begins

Body language

  • Stand upright, alert and open. Slouching or crossed arms signal disengagement
  • Move with purpose: attentive, never frantic
  • Phones never visible in guest-facing areas
Your presentation says something to every guest before you say a word. Make sure it says: I'm proud to be here, and I'm here for you.

The venue as a reflection of us

Every team member is responsible for the whole venue, not just their own area. As a manager, walk it with this list in your head.

What we're always looking for
  • Is the venue clean: floors, surfaces, seating, restrooms?
  • Are tables and chairs free of crumbs, stains and residue?
  • Are back-of-house items (radios, supplies, equipment) out of guest sight?
  • Is all signage and in-venue collateral straight, current and presentable?
  • Are courts clean, barriers straight and balls collected?
  • Do the café counter, pastry display and condiment station look guest-ready?
  • Is anything out of place anywhere in the venue?
See it, do it. If you walk past a napkin on the floor, a chair out of place or a surface that needs a wipe, take care of it right now. Cleanliness and presentation aren't housekeeping tasks. They're hospitality.

Friendly & enthusiastic

Warmth is the first step in hospitality. Not scripted, not performative. Just real.

How we show warmth
  • Approach every guest with a real smile
  • Show your personality. When you're having fun, guests feel it
  • Stay friendly even when it's hard. Professionalism means consistency
  • Be fully present with a guest: set everything else aside
  • Open, positive body language. Guests read you before you speak
When guests are leaving
  • Send every guest off warmly: “Hope you had an amazing time! See you soon!”
  • Give a positive send-off rather than open-ended questions they may not want to answer
  • The goodbye is the last impression. Make it as strong as the first

Language & communication

We work in a specialized environment with its own language, and guests don't speak it. Always use familiar, conversational language.

Instead of saying…Say this instead
“We're 86 on that”“We're actually out of that today. Can I suggest something else?”
“Court 3 is being turned”“Your court will be ready in just a few minutes”
“Two-top / four-top”“A table for two / a table for four”
“The host desk is slammed”“We're a bit busy right now. I'll be right with you”
“RallyTrack isn't registering”“Let me take a look at your screen and help get that sorted”
“The BEO says…”“Based on what we have planned for your group…”
Radio awareness. Guests can hear radios. Turn yours down immediately when you're near a guest, keep volume low on the floor at all times, and step away from guests before transmitting if possible.
General communication standards
  • Speak clearly and confidently. A mumbled greeting doesn't land
  • Use guests' names when you know them
  • No slang, profanity or overly casual language on the floor, even with teammates
  • Never speak negatively about a guest, a teammate or Rally within earshot of anyone
  • Don't know the answer? “Great question, let me find out for you right now.”
