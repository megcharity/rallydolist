Rally Training

Selling at Rally

Nobody books a court because they're hungry. That's where we come in.

Selling isn't about being pushy. It's about being prepared, present, specific, and making it effortless for a guest to say yes.
Part 1 · Know Your Product

Selling starts with knowing your product.

You cannot confidently sell something you don't understand. Before you learn how to sell at Rally, you need to know what you're selling.

Know what it tastes like

Be able to describe our food and drinks in your own words, not just repeat the menu description.

Know what you recommend

Have favorites you can sell with conviction and explain why you like them.

Know the alternatives

Be ready with beer, wine, cocktails, coffee, smoothies and non-alcoholic options for different guests.

You don't need to memorize a sales pitch. You need to know the product well enough that you don't need one.

Test yourself

Could you confidently answer these questions right now?

“What's your favorite cocktail?”   “What's your favorite food item?”   “I don't drink. What should I get?”

If you can't answer without looking at the menu, that's where your selling training starts.

Part 2 · Why We Sell

Selling is hospitality.

Rally guests arrive thinking about pickleball, friends, competition, or the event they came to attend. Food and drinks happen around that experience. Our job is to notice the opportunity, make a genuine recommendation, and make ordering easy.

THE GUEST

A better experience

A cold drink between games or food after the last point can turn a court reservation into a full night out.

YOU

A stronger check

Thoughtful recommendations build the check and can build your tips.

RALLY

A stronger business

Food and beverage revenue supports the venue, courts, and team.

Sell like a friend who works here: genuine, excited, confident, and true.
Part 3 · Read the Court

There are three selling windows.

Great Captains don't interrupt constantly. They recognize when guests are most open to hearing from them. Click each window.

WINDOW 1

Before first serve

Arrival, check-in and warm-up.

Your biggest setup opportunity.

Build the relationship, get the first drink order from each person, set the check-in plan and plant the food idea.

WINDOW 2

Between games

Water breaks, rotations and natural pauses.

Fast and specific.

Use 10-second check-ins, refill before empty and keep water moving.

WINDOW 3

After the last point

The final game and walk off court.

The most-missed sale.

Guests are hungry, thirsty and finished playing. Invite them to stay and make the next step easy.

You make the call

A group is in a tight game at 9–9. One player's drink is empty. What do you do?

Part 4 · Break the Barrier

Win the first 30 seconds.

The greeting determines whether guests see you as part of their experience or an interruption. Your goal is to feel like their person at Rally.

1
Warm energy.

A real smile. Be glad they're here.

2
Introduce yourself.

Give your name and learn theirs when you can.

3
Get comfortable.

Meet them at eye level and ask whether it's their first time.

4
Recommend, don't recite.

Point out a couple of things and lead with one drink and one food item you genuinely like.

5
Ask every person.

Don't let one guest answer for the whole group.

6
Set the plan.

Tell them you'll check in between games so your return feels expected, not intrusive.

The structure matters more than the script: welcome → name → real recommendation → ask every person → set the check-in plan.
Part 5 · Language That Sells

Stop making “no” easy.

Vague yes-or-no questions create vague no's. Be specific. Offer choices. Make recommendations.

Make it feel like part of Rally

Help guests understand that food and drinks are part of the Rally experience. Natural social proof makes a recommendation feel easy and familiar.

Try:

“People love getting [shareable item] for the court. It’s perfect to snack on between games.”
“Our NA Spritzes are also delicious if you want something fun without alcohol.”

Keep it true. Never invent popularity or exaggerate what other guests are ordering.

“Are you good?”
→
“Can I get you started with a beer or a smoothie?”
“Do you want anything to drink?”
→
“What are we drinking today?”
“Do you want to see a food menu?”
→
“Have you had our pizza yet? I have a favorite.”
“Let me know if you need anything.”
→
“I'll swing back at the end of this game.”
“Anything else?”
→
“Should I get another round going for your next game?”

Part 6 · Stay Present

Check in often. Don't hover.

Circulate

Keep moving through your section. Guests should see you even when you aren't stopping.

Read the empties

A drink getting low is your cue. Don't wait for the guest to hunt you down.

Never walk empty-handed

Carry water, clear empties and bring what the court needs on every pass.

Use eye contact

A smile and nod can be a check-in without interrupting play.

Commit to your return

“I'll be back at the end of this game.” Then keep your word.

Two-bite check

Once food lands, return after a couple of bites. Catch problems and look for the next opportunity.

Part 7 · Don't Interrupt the Game

Sell smarter, not less.

Court safety comes first.

Never walk onto an active court or behind a baseline during a point. Wait for play to stop, stay aware of the court, and use natural pauses.

Learn the rhythm.

End of a game. Water break. Rotation. Stray ball. These are selling windows. Watch the game so you know when one is coming.

Get permission early.

At the greet, tell guests you'll check in between games. Now your later visits feel expected instead of intrusive.

Sell to the person who isn't playing.

Players waiting to rotate, spectators and organizers are easier to engage because they aren't mid-game.

Work around the game.

Offer to time food for a break or the end of the session. Guests don't have to stop playing, and food is ready when they are.

Read the group.

If they're locked into a close game, wait. If they're laughing between points, make the quick offer. The same group can change minute to minute.

You make the call

You approach between games and the group quickly says, “We're good, thanks.” What's the best move?

Part 8 · Know Who You're Selling To

Different guests need different approaches.

Court reservation groups

They're here to have fun together and may be celebrating. Think rounds, shareable food and the post-session “stay and hang” invitation.

Open play / drop-in

They're rotating, meeting people and focused on getting games. The bench is your opportunity. Sell while they're waiting to rotate.

League players

They are competitive, distracted and often arrive straight from work. Learn names and usual orders. Keep offers binary and fast, then sell the post-match hang.

Spectators & hangers

They aren't playing. They have time to eat and drink. Don't overlook them while focusing on the court.

Corporate & event groups

Corporate groups are one of our biggest selling opportunities. Guests are often ordering on a company card, so the organizer is usually focused on making sure everyone has a great experience rather than keeping every individual order small.

Keep selling throughout the event. Suggest another round, replenish food before it runs out, and look for opportunities to add more shareables.

Work through the organizer, understand the plan, dietary needs and timing, and make their job easy. Your goal: make the host look like a hero while keeping the group taken care of.

Part 9 · Responsible Selling

Trust is worth more than one sale.

Water always

Our guests are exercising. Offer water consistently, especially in the heat.

Take “no” gracefully

One confident offer is hospitality. Repeating the same offer after a clear no is pestering.

Bold and true

Never invent scarcity, popularity or claims. Confidence works only when the recommendation is honest.

Alcohol requires judgment

Follow Rally's ID and responsible-service procedures. If a guest appears over-intoxicated, stop offering alcohol and involve a manager.

Part 10 · The Rally Steps of Selling

Every group. Every session.

1
Greet fast.

Smile, introduce yourself, welcome the group and connect.

2
Feature one drink + one food item.

Match the recommendation to the moment, weather and group.

3
Ask every person.

Get an individual drink response instead of letting one person answer for everyone.

4
Set the check-in plan.

Tell guests when you'll be back and how to get you sooner.

5
Plant the food.

Offer to time it around their play.

6
Own the between-game windows.

Refill before empty. Water always. Make fast, specific offers.

7
Food drop + two-bite check.

Make sure everything is right and look for the next opportunity.

8
Make the last-game pitch.

Invite them to stay instead of letting the parking lot make the decision.

9
Meaningful goodbye.

Use names when you know them, thank them and invite them back.
