HotSchedules for Managers

Every associate learns the same line over and over in their own HotSchedules training: "nothing is official until a manager approves it." This chapter is the other half — what you're actually checking for, and how to build and run the schedule itself.

The standard

Every request sits in a queue until you act on it. Letting it sit isn't neutral — it leaves someone's week unresolved.

Building & publishing

The schedule starts with you, not a request queue.

  1. Build against labour targets first

    Check your labour goals before you start placing shifts, not after. It's much easier to build it right than to fix it after it's posted.

  2. Publish at least one week ahead

    Gives the team time to request changes before the week starts, instead of scrambling once it's live.

  3. Check availability on file before you place anyone

    Don't schedule around an outdated availability — if it looks wrong, confirm with them before you build around it.

Approving availability changes

A standing weekly pattern — this changes what you can schedule them for going forward, not just one shift.

  • Check it against the shifts they're already scheduled for after the change takes effect — approving it doesn't undo what's already posted
  • Make sure it still leaves you enough coverage for their role, especially on weekends or peak shifts
  • If it creates a real coverage gap, talk to them before you deny it outright — there's often a version of it that works for both sides
  • Don't sit on it. An undecided availability request makes it hard for them to plan their life, and for you to plan the next schedule

Approving time-off requests

A one-time date, not a standing pattern.

  • Approve early requests early — the earlier it's decided, the easier it is to schedule around
  • Weigh it against who else is already off that day and what the event calendar looks like
  • If you have to deny it, tell them directly and explain why, don't just leave it pending or let it expire

Drops, swaps & pickups

The associate side is already a two-step handoff — a coworker has to accept first. You're the final check.

What to check before you approve
  • Does it create overtime, for either person?
  • Is the person picking it up actually trained for that role?
  • Does it leave the shift appropriately covered — not just filled?
What you're approving
  • Drop: they're giving the shift away — it's released to the team or one coworker
  • Swap: a straight trade, already accepted by the other person
  • Pickup: someone claiming a dropped shift, or a house shift you posted
A dropped shift is still theirs until someone picks it up and you approve it. That's exactly what the associate guide tells them — if it's sitting unpicked close to the shift, follow up with the original person rather than assuming it's covered.

Posting a house shift

An open shift with no one assigned — yours to post when you need extra coverage.

  • Post it as soon as you know you need it — more notice means more people can grab it
  • It shows up in Available Pickup right alongside dropped shifts, so anyone can request it
  • You still approve the pickup, same as any other request
