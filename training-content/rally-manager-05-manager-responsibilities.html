Manager
Responsibilities

Being a manager at Rally means owning the full shift, not just the floor. Every shift has non-negotiable duties that make sure the venue is ready, the team is set up to succeed, and leadership has full visibility.

The Rally Standard

Managers run the floor and the business.

What you'll cover

AM opening

  • Opening checklist and readiness sign-off
  • Cash, BEOs, roster and lineup
  • Handing off to PM

PM closing

  • KDS and event POCs
  • Closing walkthrough
  • Cash, shift report and lock-up

Weekly & monthly

  • Scheduling and ordering
  • Labour and sales reviews
  • Inventory and P&L

AM Opening Manager

  1. Follow the opening checklistGet the venue ready for service. It should be guest-ready 15 minutes before opening.→ Opening Checklist (SharePoint)
  2. Pre-shift walkthrough: sign offComplete the venue readiness checklist before service and sign your name. If it's not signed, it didn't happen.→ Venue Readiness Checklist (SharePoint)
  3. Count cash drawersCount all cash drawers and the café bar. Verify opening amounts, record them in the cash log, and escalate any discrepancy immediately.→ Cash Handling SOP (SharePoint)
  4. Review BEOs and meet with the kitchen before 9amReview all BEOs for the day, align with the kitchen on every event, and ring all BEO items into Toast before service.
  5. Print the roster and floor chartPrint the day's roster from HotSchedules. Create and post the floor chart so everyone knows their role and section.
  6. Fill out and print the lineup sheetBefore associates arrive: events, activations, sales targets, 86'd items, and anything else the team needs to know.→ Lineup Sheet Template (SharePoint)
  7. Review the lineup with every associate as they arriveEach associate comes to the bar for a one-on-one review of the game plan, their role, activations and sales targets. Every associate, every shift, no exceptions.
  8. Be on the floor: coach, support, motivateStay visible. Coach in real time, support the café and bar, run competitions, motivate the team to sell.
  9. Do your Figure of 8sKeep the loop going all shift (see below).
  10. Close out the café and note paper goodsConfirm the café is clean and closed out. Pass any low paper goods to the PM manager.
  11. Complete the AM shift report and hand offFill out the AM block, count reservations to your cutoff, and write a clear handoff note for the PM manager.→ Chapter 6 · Shift Report
Don't leave without completing your shift report.

PM Closing Manager

  1. Pre-shift walkthrough: sign offWhen you arrive, complete the venue readiness checklist before taking over, and sign off.→ Venue Readiness Checklist (SharePoint)
  2. Review the roster and floor chartAdjust for PM and make sure every team member knows their position.
  3. Review the lineup with every associate as they arriveTonight's game plan, events, activations and sales targets. Every associate, every shift.→ Lineup Sheet Template (SharePoint)
  4. Be on the floor: coach, support, motivateStay visible all evening. Coach, recognize great work, run competitions.
  5. Do your Figure of 8sNo empty plates, no uncollected glasses, food moving from the window.
  6. Check the KDS: monitor ticket timesCheck the Kitchen Display System regularly and address long ticket times with the kitchen immediately.
  7. Touch base with every event POCBefore any event guest leaves, find the POC in person and get feedback. Fill in the Events section of the shift report right after.→ Chapter 6 · Shift Report
  8. Flag bar shortages to leadershipIf anything at the bar is critically low, tell the GM before it becomes a floor problem.
  9. Note paper goods that are lowCheck all paper goods before close and note anything low in the shift report.
  10. Follow the closing checklistWork through it with the full team. Nothing gets skipped.→ Closing Checklist (SharePoint)
  11. Closing walkthrough with the full teamBefore anyone leaves, walk every area with the closing team: bathrooms, tables, outdoor area, floors, courts, bar, café. Everyone participates.
  12. Confirm every check is paid and everyone is clocked outNo open tabs left overnight. Every team member clocked out before they leave.
  13. Collect shift reviews from all associatesCollect printed shift reviews (printed from Toast) from every associate before they leave.
  14. Count bar, café and cash drawersRecord cash drops and tips on the cash sheet, log everything, and escalate any discrepancy.→ Cash Handling SOP (SharePoint)
  15. Complete the PM shift report and send the Day SummaryFill out the full PM block, pull sales and covers from Toast, complete the Events section, then screenshot the Day Summary and email the team.→ Chapter 6 · Shift Report
  16. Lock up: lights, TVs, music offFinal sweep. Leave the venue the way you'd want to open it.
Last one out owns it.

The Figure of 8

Your loop through the venue, repeated all shift.

  1. Indoor courtsWalk the indoor courts and introduce yourself to guests.
  2. Outdoor courtsCheck the outdoor courts and lounges.
  3. ExpoWalk to expo. Look for empty plates, glasses and food sitting in the window. Then start the loop again.
Run PULSE on every loop: playlists, upkeep, lighting, screens, environment (Chapter 4).

Case by case

When the situation calls for it.

Incident reporting

  • Any incident involving a guest, team member or the venue gets documented on the shift report
  • Escalate serious incidents to leadership immediately. Don't wait until the end of the shift
  • If it's not documented, it didn't happen

Performance write-ups

  • Follow Chapter 8 · Coaching & Corrective Action
  • Complete the form when formal documentation is required
  • Always have a witness present
  • File the form and note the conversation in the shift report

Own the problem

When something goes wrong (equipment, a guest issue, a gap in service), you don't just report it. You take ownership and take steps to solve it.

  1. Own itIt's your shift, so it's your problem. Don't wait for someone else to notice or pick it up.
  2. Take actionDo everything you can to fix it, or at least contain it, right now. Look for a workaround that keeps the guest experience on track.
  3. Escalate with a planIf it needs payment approval, reach out to the GM, but bring what you've already done and what you recommend. Get it as far as you can first.
  4. Close the loopFollow through until it's actually solved, and note what happened and what you did in the shift report.
Problem solvers are huge at Rally. “The ice machine is down” is a report. “The ice machine is down. I called the repair company, they can come at 2pm for a $150 service fee, and I've bought bagged ice to get us through. Can you approve the service call?” is ownership.

Weekly & monthly

Weekly
  • Scheduling: build and publish in HotSchedules at least one week ahead. Review labour targets first
  • Bar ordering: review pars, check stock, place the order
  • Café ordering: review pars, coordinate with the café team
  • Paper goods and supplies: check and replenish
  • Equipment and cleaning check: flag maintenance needs and confirm the cleaning schedule was followed
  • Labour tracking review: flag overages and patterns
  • Captain sales review: top performers and anyone who needs coaching
  • Weekly managers meeting: all managers plus leadership. Come prepared
Monthly
  • Inventory count: full end-of-month count for bar and café, submitted to leadership by the 1st
  • Deep clean and maintenance review: confirm the deep clean, walk the venue, submit a maintenance report
  • P&L review with leadership. Come with context on variances: labour, comps, sales
